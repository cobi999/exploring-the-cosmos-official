What Is the Universe Expanding Into?

If space itself is expanding, what lies beyond it?

EXPLORING THE COSMOS

What Is the Universe Expanding Into?

One of the most interesting questions about the universe concerns its expansion. Over the years, scientists and great physicists have discovered that the universe is expanding. This is because they observed that, on large scales, distant galaxies tend to move away from one another. But one may ask: how did they come up with this information?

Through numerous observations, scientists discovered that light from many distant galaxies is stretched towards longer, redder wavelengths. This is called redshift, and it tells us that space has expanded while the light travelled towards us.

So, What Is the Universe Expanding Into?

According to our everyday understanding, something expands into the space outside it. Since space itself is expanding, does that mean there is another space outside our space that our space is expanding into?

Well, here is the twist: the standard model of cosmology does not require the universe to have an outside or an edge that it expands into. Space can expand without expanding into something else. That sounds crazy, right? It may seem to suggest that the universe is infinite, although expansion alone does not prove that the universe is infinite. It could also be finite without having an edge.

The universe expanding does not mean that every galaxy is moving away from every other galaxy. For instance, we have the Andromeda Galaxy, which is moving towards the Milky Way. If space itself is expanding, shouldn't every galaxy move away from every other galaxy?

Well, one thing to understand is that cosmic expansion becomes most significant on very large scales. On local scales, things can be different because of forces such as gravity. Nearby galaxies can attract one another strongly enough to move towards each other despite cosmic expansion. The Milky Way and Andromeda belong to the Local Group, a collection of galaxies bound together by gravity. Their mutual gravitational attraction dominates their relative motion.

Is Everything in the Universe Expanding?

This leads us to an even more interesting question: if the universe is expanding, is everything inside it also expanding?

The answer is no. The Solar System is not expanding with the universe in the same way, and neither is your body. Gravity and other forces hold many nearby structures together against cosmic expansion.

The universe has been expanding for about 13.8 billion years, since its early hot, dense state. Observations also indicate that its expansion is currently accelerating.

So, if the expansion is accelerating, does it mean that there is something fuelling it? Scientists often use the term dark energy to describe whatever is responsible for this accelerated expansion, although its true physical nature remains uncertain.

And what happens when the fuel runs out? Is dark energy something that can be exhausted, or will the universe continue expanding forever?

Perhaps the most fascinating thing about the universe is that every answer seems to open the door to another question.
